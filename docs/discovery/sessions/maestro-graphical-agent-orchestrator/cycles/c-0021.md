## What changed, and why it is larger than one node

Three things landed in one cycle, and the second caused the third.

### 1. Requirements have priorities now

Until this cycle, priority existed only on discovery **nodes**. No requirement
carried one, so every reading of "the P0 set" was re-derived from prose and could
differ between cycles. The user re-ranked the entire index directly, keeping 16
items at P0 and demoting the rest.

The loop pushed back on one part of the re-rank and was right to: several demoted
items are not independent scope but the **mechanisms** of retained P0s. `parentId`
exclusion is the correctness of the subagent tree; Liveness-from-evidence is what
restart recomputes; process-group ownership is what teardown is made of. They are
recorded as a `P0-implied` class rather than as a P1 backlog, because listing a
mechanism beside the requirement it implements invites deferring the mechanism and
keeping the promise.

A second correction was the loop's own: `N0.20`-`N0.27` are **verification method**,
not product scope, and had been ranked alongside product capability by mistake when
the index was first derived.

**Nord was added as a new P0** (`F0.27`) and costs nothing on the chosen route -
cmux ships it in six variants.

### 2. The founding requirement was narrowed by its owner

`N0.1` - *no Agent process may outlive the application* - is the requirement this
whole product was created to satisfy, and it came from measured forensics in c-0006:
a detached `herdr server` daemon holding two Copilot Sessions and five Model Context
Protocol servers alive for **two days** past app exit.

The user proposed scratching it: *"surely it must not be a real issue?"* The loop
presented the evidence rather than complying - c-0006's forensics, c-0012's 9-vs-0
survivors, the macOS responsible-process trust defect, and the independent ccmux and
Warp reproductions - and then offered a split the requirement had been hiding:
**verified-zero-at-quit** (expensive) versus **sweep-on-launch** (cheap, survives a
crash, and is what actually bounds the two-day daemon).

The user took the split, then went one step further and declined escalation:
*"So long as cmux does a best attempt at shutting stuff down, that's OK. We don't
have to force kill processes and stuff, that's unnecessary."* The loop put the one
contradicting measurement back once - c-0012 measured `SIGTERM` alone stalling at
**5 survivors** for three seconds on a live Session, which is precisely the outcome
the user said they did not want - and the user confirmed: *"best effort is totally
fine."*

**Settled:** best-effort teardown at quit, sweep-on-launch at P0, verified-zero and
escalation at P1. The c-0006 forensics are **not** withdrawn. The residual risk is
now *accepted* rather than engineered out, and that distinction is recorded rather
than smoothed over. This is the second reversal of a c-0006 requirement: c-0009
falsified its spawn model by measurement, c-0021 narrows its teardown guarantee by
decision.

### 3. The narrowing changed the loop's own recommendation mid-cycle

Q1 recommended **forking** cmux, because teardown-with-verification needs source
access. When the user removed that bar, the recommendation's entire justification
disappeared, and the loop withdrew it in favour of **wrapping** rather than carrying
a recommendation whose reason had been deleted. Q4 re-asked the question honestly and
the user chose `wrap it`.

## Prototype n-0011-c-0021

Approved with the exact string. Isolation path removed and verified gone in the same
cycle. **cmux pinned at 0.64.22 (102) [ddd4a01bc]**; the Homebrew cask declares
`auto_updates`, so the pin is the only anchor these measurements have.

**Hypothesis confirmed.** Stock cmux plus an external read-only helper presents a
Fleet - enforced worktree and branch, and a live subagent tree rendered inside cmux -
with no source changes and no writes to shared configuration.

**The decisive measurement.** cmux refuses external socket control by default
(`automation.socketControlMode: cmuxOnly`), and the boundary is not all-or-nothing:
`cmux <path>` **succeeds** from outside, while any query or control is denied. A
helper started **inside a cmux pane** is fully privileged. Confirmed visually - the
metadata pill `0 agents / 0 running` and the status line `Fleet alpha - Maestro
attached`, both written by an external Python process, render in cmux's own sidebar.

**This settles the architecture:** no fork, no Swift, no `socketPassword`, no
loosening of cmux's security posture.

**cmux already ships what this session had specified as work.**
`top --processes --sort cpu|mem` is `F0.24`; `right-sidebar files` is `F0.20`;
workspace selection is `F0.9`; tabs and splits are `F1.3`; Attention exists and is
better than our design; Nord is built in. And `read-screen` + `send` +
`workspace list` make cmux **the most automatable host measured so far**, which
inverts part of the ranking the c-0015 automation criterion implied - measured, not
researched.

## Findings that qualify earlier cycles

- **Subagent nesting reaches depth 3.** c-0010 and c-0020 both recorded a maximum of
  2. Seven local sessions nest; one reaches depth 3 with zero unresolved. The
  requirement survives; the figure does not, and any assertion calibrated to depth 2
  is calibrated wrong.
- **`inuse.<pid>.lock` is a liveness signal**, previously unrecorded anywhere in this
  session's state. Its verdict agreed with `ps`.
- **`N0.5` was made false and was rewritten, not demoted.** *No third-party dependency
  may supply the process lifetime* is exactly what hosting inside cmux does. The rule
  is now: a dependency may supply the lifetime **when that lifetime is visible to and
  endable by the operator**. `herdr` was dangerous because it was hidden, not because
  it owned processes.
- **`N0.11`, `N0.18`, `N0.19` are retired, not demoted** - they were v2 Electron
  artifacts, and return only if Maestro ever ships as its own bundle.

## The harness lied twice, silently

Evidence selection broke the negative control twice without failing. Selecting by
**file size** picked a 195 MB session whose read window held one subagent; selecting
by **subagent count** picked a 58-subagent session that was entirely **flat**, where
the forbidden `parentId` construction *coincidentally agrees* with the correct join.
Both runs executed the control and reported PASS while testing nothing. Only after
selecting for **depth** did it fire - 2 of 6 parents wrong.

`parentId` diverges only where subagents actually nest, which is exactly why it
looked plausible for as long as it did. **Running a negative control is not the same
as controlling**, and the c-0016 lesson reproduced itself in a cycle that was
explicitly trying to honour it.
