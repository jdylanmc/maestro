## The analysis

Nine requirements are **already delivered** by the host, six are **partial**,
eleven must be **built**, and three are **moot**. The detail is in `evidence.md`.

The shape of the result matters more than the counts: what cmux gives away is
breadth - Attention, notifications, layout, cancellation, process groups,
keyboard model - and what it withholds is exactly one thing, **durable lifecycle
state**. Its four agent states are ephemeral and recomputed from hooks by
deliberate design. `Parked` versus `Interrupted` is therefore the largest single
build item on this route, and it is the reason Maestro remains software rather
than a cmux configuration file.

One adjacency no prior cycle had noticed: cmux's `maxLiveTerminals` (default 12)
and its hibernation path already implement graceful degradation under load, which
is the neighbourhood of the 8-Fleet ceiling and of admission control.

## Three decisions, two of which reframed the question asked

### The routes question closed itself

Answered unprompted, before the group opened: *"cmux joins the comparison but
push it to the front of the lead."* n-0011 does not supersede n-0004, n-0005, or
n-0006 - the `supersedes-candidate` links staged in c-0021 are **withdrawn**.
There are now **five routes**, and every "four routes" or "four reports" count
recorded since c-0011 is superseded. The live sequence is cmux, WezTerm, Tauri,
Swift, with Electron already built and reported.

This extends c-0011's evidence-order rule rather than breaking it. Electron led
because it alone carried measured evidence; cmux now carries measured evidence
too, and against the current P0 set rather than the superseded one.

### The SDK contradiction was real, and the fix was a distinction the loop had not made

The wrap route bypasses the Copilot SDK entirely - the c-0021 prototype used no
`CopilotClient`, no `sendAndWait`, no `permissions.pendingRequests()`. On its face
that stranded four cycles of SDK work and left `ADR 0002` and `N0.17` describing
an integration no longer performed.

The user resolved it by **route class** rather than by picking a winner:
*"Deprioritize the copilot seam for cmux directly since it can use the cli and
it's a terminal itself -- reserve copilot sdk for the apps where we need to create
our own chat interface."* Terminal-hosted routes drive a Fleet through the CLI,
because the terminal already *is* the chat interface. Application routes use the
SDK, because they must build one.

**c-0014 is narrowed, not falsified**, and the SDK work is preserved. The reason
it looked stranded is that the seam had been recorded as a property of *the
product* when it is a property of *the route class*. `N0.17`'s version pin binds
only where the SDK is used; a terminal-hosted route pins the event-log format
instead - the more stable of the two, since the SDK permission surface changed
shape across three observed versions while the log join has held across four
cycles and 36,000+ events.

### Park was decorative, and a principle arrived with the fix

`F0.13` (auto-Park) and `F0.14` (relaunch restores) sat at P0 while `F0.15`
(Parked vs Interrupted) and `F0.16` (two axes) sat at P1 - so on relaunch every
Fleet would look identical, which is precisely the outcome c-0008 rejected silent
termination to avoid, in that word. Sharper here than it would have been on
Electron, because cmux holds no durable intent at all. **F0.15 and F0.16 move to
`P0-implied`.**

The answer carried a new requirement with it: *"we basically want to 'lean in' on
each platform and embrace it's strengths."* A route implements the contract in its
host's idiom rather than reproducing a design authored for another host.

**This immediately corrected a defect in this cycle's own analysis.** cmux's
global, chronological notification Feed had been scored a *partial miss* against
"selecting a Fleet re-scopes every panel". Under the principle it is not a miss -
a unified triage inbox is a legitimate expression of Attention across 8 Fleets.
Also settled: the layout is cmux's, not the c-0005 wireframe, and subagents may
render as panes as well as a tree.

**The principle is bounded, and the bound is load-bearing.** The Acceptance Slice
stays identical across routes and the report keeps its fixed five-section shape.
Idiomatic *implementation* is permitted; idiomatic *behaviour* and an idiomatic
*rubric* are not. Without that bound, "lean in" silently becomes "each route is
judged by its own standard", which would void the five-route comparison confirmed
in this same cycle. The rubric question becomes "what does our design become on
this stack" rather than "can this stack implement our design".
